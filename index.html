🌍 CiviStories: A Global Cry for Digital Mercy

From the heart of Egypt, where a thousand cases of divorce are recorded annually due to screen misuse.
And where billions of pounds are wasted between productivity losses, electronic fraud, and psychological treatment.
We raise our voice not just as Egyptians, but as humans who see that humanity is undergoing a new test.

The goal of this platform is not to accuse technology, but to save human relations from it.

We do not ask for a ban on phones, but we call for using them with a conscience.

Every child in Tokyo or Tripoli, in Sao Paulo or Seoul, deserves to look into their mother's eyes without a screen separating them.
Every young person in Cape Town or Kuala Lumpur deserves to build their identity from their reality, not from the illusion of algorithms.

Social media is not evil, but it is not neutral either; it is a mirror of the intentions of those who direct it.
Today, if not directed by mercy, it may become the fastest way to dismantle families, sow despair, and push some to suicide.

Therefore, CiviStories launches:
A human appeal open to every government, religious, cultural, or civil institution to participate in building a digital space that protects dignity, not threatens it.

We started in Egypt — because honesty starts at home.
But our message is not for Egypt alone, but for the entire world.

✨ What we ask for today:
- From parents: To return to being the first "filter" for their children.
- From governments: To set policies that protect children from digital addiction.
- From platforms: To choose humans over profit.
- From spiritual and cultural institutions: To guide the conscience, not to remain silent.
- Every reference in the world — religious, philosophical, or human —
To join this cry, with its language, values, and vision.

> Share our message and save those you love. Protect humanity before it becomes a digital memory.
> Do not let the screen steal your child's childhood, your home's peace, or your hope in life.

Removing Digital Harm: A Jain Vision for Protecting the Human Journey

Jainism is based on the principles of absolute non-violence (Ahimsa) and spiritual liberation. In the digital age, where virtual space has become a field of existence and interaction, there is an urgent need to apply Jain principles to protect souls (Jiva) from new forms of harm.

First: Non-violence (Ahimsa) in Digital Space
Ahimsa in Jainism is not harming in all forms: by body, speech, and thought. The digital world presents a triple challenge:

Level of Ahimsa Digital Challenge Proposed Jain Practice
Physical Sensory fatigue, sleep disturbance Setting usage times, rest periods
Verbal Hate speech, bullying, defamation Truthful, kind, and beneficial speech
Intellectual Hatred, malice, hidden resentment Monitoring thoughts, purifying intention

Every soul (Jiva) is my dear being (Lord Mahavira); this includes the souls we meet digitally.

Second: The Five Great Vows (Mahavratas) in the Digital Age

  1. Ahimsa (Non-violence):
    • Not publishing content that causes psychological or spiritual harm
    • Protecting vulnerable users from harmful content
    • Avoiding participation in hate speech or marginalization
  2. Satya (Truth):
    • Verifying information before publishing
    • Publicly correcting errors
    • Refusing to create or publish fake news
  3. Asteya (Non-stealing):
    • Respecting digital intellectual property
    • Not impersonating identities or content
    • Reducing the digital divide (justice in access to info)
  4. Brahmacharya (Chastity):
    • Discipline in digital usage (addiction as non-chastity)
    • Avoiding exploitative or harmful sexual content
    • Maintaining intellectual and spiritual purity
  5. Aparigraha (Non-possession):
    • Liberation from attachment to digital identity
    • Reducing unnecessary digital footprint
    • Simplicity in usage and communication

Third: Cleanliness of Speech (Bhasha Samiti)
Lord Mahavira set precise rules for speech, applied digitally:

  1. Speech must be:
    • Truthful (no misleading or faking)
    • Useful (no nonsense or time wasting)
    • Kind (not hurtful or harmful)
    • Specific (no ambiguity or vagueness)
  2. Reflection before digital posting:
    • Is this the whole truth?
    • Will it benefit the receiver?
    • Does it express kindness and respect?
    • Is it clear and not subject to misunderstanding?

Fourth: The Concept of Digital Karma
In Jainism, Karma is a subtle matter that sticks to the soul. Digital actions generate new karma:

Digital Action Type of Karma Spiritual Impact
Posting lies Karma of Ignorance (Mohaniya) Obscures truth, reinforces illusion
Hate speech Karma of negative emotions Reinforces anger, hate, attachment
Digital addiction Karma of attachment (Mishrya) Binds the soul to the material world
Digital assistance Karma of virtue (Punya) Relieves previous karma

Fifth: Digital Fasting and Discipline
As Jains practice physical fasting, it is suggested:

  1. Media Fasting:
    • Days without social media
    • Daily digital silence periods
    • Periodic news fasting
  2. Viewing Discipline:
    • Awareness of what we consume digitally
    • Choosing content that uplifts the soul
    • Avoiding violent or exploitative content
  3. Purifying Digital Environment:
    • As we clean our physical homes
    • Cleaning toxic contacts
    • Purifying information sources

Practical Jain Recommendations

  1. Daily practice of the triple check before digital interaction:
    • Check Body: Is my posture healthy? Are eyes rested?
    • Check Speech: Are my words true, useful, kind?
    • Check Thought: Is my intent pure? Are my thoughts free of hate?
  2. Creating safe digital havens:
    • Groups based on mutual respect
    • Platforms committed to Ahimsa principles
    • Spaces for tolerant dialogue

One soul suffers and one soul is liberated in every moment (Jain Text)

Every digital interaction is a chance to practice Ahimsa or violate it. The digital space as a new arena for existence calls us to apply Jain discipline with greater precision because the impact of our digital actions may be wider and faster than ever.

The path toward liberation (Moksha) passes through the digital space today as well. Removing digital harm is not just a social responsibility but a spiritual necessity for anyone seeking purity and liberation.

Removing Harm in the Digital Age: African Traditional Philosophy Vision for Protecting the Human Journey

           

                In traditional African philosophies, the universe is an organic interconnected whole, human relationships are sacred, and collective wisdom is the foundation. Modern digital space is tested through the lens of Ubuntu (unity in plurality), Ujanjo (common humanity), and the necessity of maintaining Iko (cosmic harmony).            

           

First: Digital Space as a New Arena for the Community
            I am because we are, and we are because I am - African Proverb.             In the African vision, identity is both individual and collective.             Digital Space:             - May strengthen community by connecting the scattered and preserving heritage.             - May weaken community if used for division and gossip (Sambia).             - Must serve Ujanjo (our shared humanity) and not lose it.             Traditional Wisdom:             Every word spoken is heard by ancestors and those not yet born. Digital does not cancel this responsibility; it expands it.

Fifth: African Concept of Time and Digital Speed
            Time is not money, Time is relationship - African Vision.             Digital speed conflicts with organic African time where:             - Time is cyclical, not linear.             - Value is in depth, not speed.             - Relationships need natural time.            

           

Proposed Practice:
            African Digital Pause - Stopping to restore the rhythm of true relationships.            

           

Conclusion: Returning to Roots through Tech
            When an old man dies in Africa, a whole library burns - Amadou Hampâté Bâ.             Digital space is a historical opportunity to preserve Africa's living libraries before they burn.             But this preservation must be:             - Respecting cultural contexts.             - With the participation of indigenous communities.             - With awareness that technology is a means, not an end.            

           

The tree that survives the storm has deep roots.
